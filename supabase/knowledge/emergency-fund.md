An emergency fund is money set aside for unplanned costs or a loss of income: a job loss, a medical bill, an urgent repair. Its purpose is to avoid borrowing at high cost or selling long-term investments at a bad time.

## How the size is worked out

Start from essential monthly spending, not total spending: rent or home loan EMI, food, utilities, other loan EMIs, insurance premiums, transport, school fees. Then choose how many months to cover. Three to six months is a commonly used range; people with irregular income, one earner in the household, or dependants often aim higher.

## A worked example

Essential monthly spending:

- Rent: ₹15,000
- Groceries and utilities: ₹12,000
- Loan EMI: ₹8,000
- Insurance and transport: ₹5,000
- Total: ₹40,000 a month

Three months is ₹1,20,000; six months is ₹2,40,000. Saving ₹10,000 a month, reaching ₹2,40,000 takes 24 months.

## Where it is usually kept

The money needs to be available quickly and to hold its value, so it is usually kept somewhere it can be withdrawn within a day or two without a loss. Returns matter less than access and safety.

## Keeping it working

- Use it only for real emergencies, and refill it after using it.
- Review the target when spending changes: a new EMI, a move, a new dependant.
- Health and life insurance reduce how much of a large shock the fund has to absorb.
