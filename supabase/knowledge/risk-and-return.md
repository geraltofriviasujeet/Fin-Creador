In investing, return is what an investment earns; risk is the chance that the outcome differs from what was expected, including losing money. Investments that can earn more over long periods usually come with bigger swings along the way.

## Kinds of risk

- Market risk: prices of shares, bonds and other assets move with the economy and sentiment.
- Credit risk: a borrower (for example, a bond issuer) may not pay interest or repay on time.
- Interest rate risk: bond prices fall when interest rates rise.
- Liquidity risk: an asset may be hard to sell quickly at a fair price.
- Inflation risk: returns may not keep up with rising prices.

## Losses and recovery

A fall needs a bigger rise to recover. ₹1,00,000 that falls 20% becomes ₹80,000; getting back to ₹1,00,000 then needs a 25% gain (₹20,000 on ₹80,000). A 50% fall needs a 100% gain to recover.

## Time horizon

Money needed soon has little time to recover from a fall, so short-term needs are usually matched with steadier assets, while money for goals many years away can ride out more ups and downs. How much fall a person could live with, both financially and emotionally, is part of the choice.

## Points to keep in mind

- A promise of high returns with no risk is a warning sign.
- Past returns do not guarantee future returns.
- Spreading money across different assets (diversification) reduces the effect of any one going wrong.
