A health insurance policy pays for medical treatment up to the sum insured, in return for a premium. These are the main terms, and the policyholder protections set by IRDAI's Master Circular on Health Insurance Business (29 May 2024).

## Key terms

- Sum insured: the most the policy pays in a policy year. A family floater shares one sum insured across the family.
- Sub-limit: a cap on a particular cost, such as room rent, below the sum insured.
- Deductible: an amount you pay yourself before the policy pays.
- Waiting period: a time after the policy starts during which specified illnesses or pre-existing conditions are not covered. Each policy states its own periods.
- No claim bonus: a reward for a claim-free year, either a higher sum insured at the same premium or a discount on the renewal premium.

## What the insurer must give you

Every policy comes with a customer information sheet that sets out, in simple words, the cover, exclusions, sub-limits, deductibles, waiting periods, the claims process and how to complain, including the Insurance Ombudsman.

## Your protections

- Free look: 30 days from receiving the policy document to review it and cancel if not satisfied (for policies of a year or more).
- Renewal: a policy cannot be refused renewal because claims were made, except for fraud, non-disclosure or misrepresentation. There is no fresh underwriting unless the sum insured is increased.
- Grace period: 15 days for monthly premiums and 30 days for quarterly, half-yearly or yearly premiums. Renewing within it keeps the credits earned (no claim bonus, waiting periods served).
- Moratorium: after 60 months of continuous cover, a policy or claim cannot be contested for non-disclosure or misrepresentation, except for established fraud.
- Portability and migration: moving to another insurer, or to another policy with the same insurer, carries over credits for the sum insured, no claim bonus, waiting periods served and the moratorium.

## Claims

- Cashless: in a network hospital, the insurer must decide on a cashless request within one hour, and give final discharge approval within three hours of the hospital's request. If it is later, any extra hospital charge is paid by the insurer.
- Documents: the insurer and its third party administrator collect them from the hospital.
- Rejection: a claim can be rejected only with the approval of the insurer's product management committee or its claims review committee, and the reasons must cite the policy terms.
- More than one policy: you can choose which policy to claim from first; that insurer coordinates with the others if its cover is not enough.

## A worked example

A family floater with a ₹5,00,000 sum insured and a room rent sub-limit of ₹5,000 a day. A 4-day stay in a ₹6,000 room: the policy pays room rent of 4 × ₹5,000 = ₹20,000 and the family pays the other ₹4,000. A policy's wording may also change other charges when the room is above its limit, so the policy document decides.
