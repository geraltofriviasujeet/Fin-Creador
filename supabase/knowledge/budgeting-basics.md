A budget is a plan for how income will be used each month: what goes to essentials, what to wants, and what to saving or paying off debt. Planning before the month starts makes it easier to save first instead of saving whatever is left.

## Steps

1. Write down monthly take-home income.
2. List fixed costs (rent, EMIs, premiums, fees) and variable costs (groceries, fuel, eating out).
3. Decide an amount for saving and debt repayment.
4. Track actual spending by category and compare it with the plan at the end of the month.
5. Adjust the next month's plan.

## One common split: 50/30/20

A widely used starting point divides take-home income into 50% for needs, 30% for wants and 20% for saving and extra debt repayment. It is a rule of thumb, not a requirement; high rent or a large EMI often pushes needs above 50%.

## A worked example

Take-home income of ₹60,000 a month:

- Needs (50%): ₹30,000
- Wants (30%): ₹18,000
- Saving and debt repayment (20%): ₹12,000

If tracking shows ₹22,000 went on wants, that is ₹4,000 over the plan; the next month's plan can either trim wants or accept a smaller saving amount knowingly.

## What helps

- Moving the saving amount out at the start of the month, on payday.
- Planning for yearly costs (insurance, school fees, festivals) by setting aside a twelfth each month.
- Keeping categories few enough to track.
