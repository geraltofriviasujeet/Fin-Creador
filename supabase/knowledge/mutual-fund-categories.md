SEBI requires mutual fund schemes to be grouped into defined categories so investors can compare like with like. Open-ended schemes fall into five broad groups.

## Equity schemes

Invest mainly in shares. Categories are defined by company size, style or theme. Company size is set by market capitalisation rank: large cap is the 1st to 100th company, mid cap the 101st to 250th, and small cap the 251st onwards. Equity-linked savings schemes (ELSS) carry a three-year lock-in. Equity schemes usually carry the highest short-term ups and downs of the groups.

## Debt schemes

Invest in bonds and money market instruments. Categories are defined by how long the investments run (from overnight and liquid funds to long duration) or by credit quality. Their value moves with interest rates and with the credit risk of the issuers.

## Hybrid schemes

Mix equity and debt in ranges set for each category, from conservative (mostly debt) to aggressive (mostly equity), plus arbitrage and other mixes.

## Solution-oriented schemes

Retirement and children's schemes, with a lock-in of at least five years or until the stated age.

## Other schemes

Index funds and exchange-traded funds, which track an index, and funds of funds, which invest in other schemes.

## Reading a category

- A category describes what a scheme may hold, not how well it will do.
- Each scheme carries a riskometer, from low to very high risk, which is reviewed regularly.
- A fund house may usually offer only one scheme per category (index funds, ETFs and some others aside).
