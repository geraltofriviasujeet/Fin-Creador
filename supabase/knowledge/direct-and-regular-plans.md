Most mutual fund schemes come in two plans: direct and regular. Both invest in exactly the same portfolio. The difference is cost.

## The difference

- A regular plan is bought through a distributor, who is paid a commission by the fund house. That commission is part of the plan's expense ratio.
- A direct plan is bought straight from the fund house or a platform that does not take a commission, so its expense ratio is lower.
- Because the expense ratio is taken out of the NAV, the two plans of one scheme have different NAVs, and the direct plan's NAV grows slightly faster.

## A worked example

₹1,00,000 invested for 10 years in a scheme whose portfolio earns 10% a year before costs:

- Direct plan, expense ratio 0.5%: net 9.5% a year, ₹1,00,000 × 1.095¹⁰ = about ₹2,47,823.
- Regular plan, expense ratio 1.5%: net 8.5% a year, ₹1,00,000 × 1.085¹⁰ = about ₹2,26,098.

The gap after 10 years is about ₹21,724. The rates and expense ratios are for illustration only.

## What the extra cost pays for

The commission pays the distributor for their service: help choosing schemes, paperwork, reviews. Someone using a direct plan does that work themselves or pays an adviser separately. Which plan suits depends on whether that service is wanted and what it is worth to the investor.

A scheme's document and fact sheet list the expense ratio of each plan.
