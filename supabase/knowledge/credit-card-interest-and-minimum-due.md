A credit card bill shows two key amounts: the total amount due and the minimum amount due. What you pay decides whether interest is charged.

## The interest-free period

Purchases are interest-free until the payment due date only if the total amount due is paid in full by that date. The time from a purchase to the due date is the interest-free period.

## Paying only the minimum

The minimum due keeps the account in good standing and avoids a late payment charge, but:

- Interest is charged on the unpaid balance, usually from the date of each purchase, not from the due date.
- New purchases usually lose the interest-free period until the balance is cleared.
- GST is charged on the interest and fees.

## A worked example

A bill of ₹20,000; ₹1,000 is paid and ₹19,000 is carried to the next month. At an illustrative 3.5% a month, interest on ₹19,000 for a month is about ₹665, before GST, and more if interest runs from the purchase dates. A rate of 3.5% a month is 42% a year.

## Cash withdrawals

Cash taken out with a credit card usually has no interest-free period and carries a fee from the day it is withdrawn.

## What the bill and the card's terms show

- The interest rate (often shown monthly) and how it is charged.
- Late payment charges, which apply only if the minimum due is not paid by the due date.
- How payments are applied to different parts of the balance.

Paying the full amount by the due date avoids interest altogether. The rate in the example is for illustration only, not any card's actual rate.
