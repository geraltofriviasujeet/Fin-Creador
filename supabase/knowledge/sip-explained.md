A systematic investment plan (SIP) invests a fixed amount in a mutual fund scheme at regular intervals, usually monthly, by automatic debit. Each instalment buys units at that day's NAV.

## Rupee cost averaging

Because the amount is fixed, each instalment buys more units when the NAV is low and fewer when it is high. Over time, the average cost per unit is below the simple average of the NAVs on the purchase dates.

## A worked example

₹5,000 a month for three months:

- Month 1, NAV ₹50: 5,000 ÷ 50 = 100 units
- Month 2, NAV ₹40: 5,000 ÷ 40 = 125 units
- Month 3, NAV ₹62.50: 5,000 ÷ 62.50 = 80 units

Total invested: ₹15,000. Total units: 305. Average cost per unit: 15,000 ÷ 305 = about ₹49.18, while the simple average of the three NAVs is about ₹50.83. At a NAV of ₹62.50 the 305 units are worth ₹19,062.50.

Averaging does not guarantee a profit or protect against a loss: if the NAV ends below the average cost, the investment is worth less than what was put in.

## Practical points

- An SIP can usually be paused, changed or stopped; each scheme's rules apply.
- Missed instalments (for example, from an empty bank account) can lead to the SIP being cancelled after repeated failures.
- An SIP is a way of investing, not a product: the risk and return come from the scheme it invests in.
