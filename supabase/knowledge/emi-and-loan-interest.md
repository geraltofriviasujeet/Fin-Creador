An equated monthly instalment (EMI) is the fixed amount paid each month on a loan. Each EMI covers that month's interest on the amount still owed, and the rest repays the loan (the principal).

## The formula

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)ⁿ ÷ ((1 + r)ⁿ − 1)

where P is the loan amount, r the monthly interest rate (the yearly rate ÷ 12), and n the number of months.

## A worked example

A loan of ₹10,00,000 at 9% a year for 5 years (60 months):

- r = 9% ÷ 12 = 0.75% a month.
- EMI = about ₹20,758.
- Total paid over 60 months: about ₹12,45,501, of which about ₹2,45,501 is interest.

In month 1, interest is ₹10,00,000 × 0.75% = ₹7,500, so ₹13,258 of the EMI repays principal. Each month the amount owed falls, so the interest part shrinks and the principal part grows.

## What changes the cost

- A longer tenure lowers the EMI but raises total interest.
- On a floating-rate loan, a change in the rate changes either the EMI or the tenure.
- Processing fees and other charges add to the cost; the annual percentage rate (APR) in the loan's key fact statement includes them.

## Before borrowing

Lenders give a key fact statement with the APR, the EMI, the number of instalments and all charges. Comparing loans on APR, not only the interest rate, shows the full cost.

The rate in the example is for illustration only.
