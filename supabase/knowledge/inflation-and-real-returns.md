Inflation is the general rise in prices over time. As prices rise, the same amount of money buys less. A return that is lower than inflation means money is growing in rupees but shrinking in what it can buy.

## Real return

The real return is the return after inflation:

(1 + return) ÷ (1 + inflation) − 1

With a 7% return and 5% inflation: 1.07 ÷ 1.05 − 1 = about 1.9% a year. Subtracting one from the other (7% − 5% = 2%) is a close shortcut at low rates.

## What inflation does over time

At 6% inflation a year:

- Things that cost ₹1,00,000 today cost about ₹1,79,085 in 10 years (₹1,00,000 × 1.06¹⁰).
- ₹1,00,000 kept as cash for 10 years buys only about ₹55,839 of today's goods (₹1,00,000 ÷ 1.06¹⁰).

## Why it matters for planning

- Goal amounts several years away (education, a home, retirement) need to be raised for inflation, not set in today's prices.
- Taxes are paid on the rupee return, so the real return after tax can be lower still.
- The inflation that matters is the person's own: school fees or medical costs can rise faster than the general index.

The rates in the examples are for illustration only, not current or expected rates.
