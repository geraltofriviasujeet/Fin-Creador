These figures are from the Income-tax Act, 2025, as published on the Income Tax Department's website, for tax year 2026-27 (1 April 2026 to 31 March 2027). A capital gain is the profit on selling or transferring a capital asset, such as shares, mutual fund units, gold or property.

## Short-term or long-term (section 2(101))

- Listed securities, units of equity-oriented funds, UTI units and zero-coupon bonds: short-term if held for 12 months or less, long-term after that.
- Other assets (for example property, gold, unlisted shares): short-term if held for 24 months or less.

## Rates

- Short-term gains on listed equity shares, equity-oriented fund units and business trust units, where securities transaction tax applies: 20% (section 196).
- Other short-term gains: added to income and taxed at the person's normal rates.
- Long-term gains on those listed equity shares and units: 12.5% on the part above ₹1,25,000 in the year (section 198).
- Other long-term gains: 12.5% (section 197). For land or buildings bought before 23 July 2024, a resident individual pays no more than 20% of the gain worked out with indexed cost (section 197(3)).

Cess and any surcharge are added on top; their rates are not covered here. For a resident individual whose other income is below the basic exemption limit, the unused part of that limit reduces the gain first.

## Worked examples (before cess)

- Equity fund units held 18 months, gain ₹2,00,000: tax on ₹2,00,000 − ₹1,25,000 = ₹75,000 at 12.5% = ₹9,375.
- Shares held 8 months, gain ₹50,000: 20% = ₹10,000.
- Gold held 3 years, gain ₹3,00,000: 12.5% = ₹37,500.

## Points to keep in mind

- The holding period runs from purchase to sale; for an SIP, each instalment has its own purchase date.
- Losses on capital assets can be set off against gains only under the Act's rules, which are not covered here.
