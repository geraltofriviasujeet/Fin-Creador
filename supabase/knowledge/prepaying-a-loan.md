Prepaying means repaying part or all of a loan before it is due. Because interest is charged on the amount still owed, paying some of it early reduces the interest paid over the rest of the loan.

## Two choices after a part-prepayment

- Keep the EMI and shorten the tenure: the same EMI clears the smaller balance sooner.
- Keep the tenure and lower the EMI: the monthly outgo falls.

Keeping the EMI usually saves more interest, because the balance falls faster.

## A worked example

A loan of ₹10,00,000 at 9% a year for 60 months has an EMI of about ₹20,758. After 12 EMIs, about ₹8,34,170 is still owed, and the remaining 48 EMIs would carry about ₹1,62,231 of interest.

A prepayment of ₹1,00,000 at that point leaves about ₹7,34,170.

- Keep the EMI: the loan ends after 42 more months instead of 48 (the last payment is about ₹5,238), and interest falls by about ₹40,070.
- Keep the tenure: the EMI falls to about ₹18,270 for 48 months, and interest falls by about ₹19,448.

## Charges

RBI does not allow banks and other regulated lenders to charge a penalty for prepaying or closing a floating-rate loan taken by an individual for a purpose other than business. Fixed-rate loans and business loans may carry a charge; the loan agreement and key fact statement say what applies.

## Things to weigh

- Keeping an emergency fund before prepaying.
- The loan's rate compared with what the money could otherwise earn, after tax.
- Any tax benefit on the loan's interest, which prepaying reduces.

The rate in the example is for illustration only.
