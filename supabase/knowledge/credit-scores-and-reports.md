A credit report is a record of your loans and credit cards: the lenders, the limits and balances, and whether each payment was made on time. A credit score is a number worked out from that report that sums up how reliably you have repaid. Lenders look at both when deciding whether to lend and at what rate.

## Who keeps them

Credit information companies (CICs) licensed by RBI collect data from banks and other lenders. There are four in India: TransUnion CIBIL, Equifax, Experian and CRIF High Mark. Each has its own scoring model; scores commonly range from 300 to 900, higher being better.

You are entitled to one free full credit report, including the score, every year from each CIC.

## What affects a score

- Repayment history: late or missed payments weigh the most.
- Credit utilisation: how much of your card limits you use. Using ₹30,000 of a ₹1,00,000 limit is 30% utilisation.
- Length of credit history.
- Mix of credit: secured loans (home, vehicle) and unsecured (cards, personal loans).
- New credit enquiries: many applications in a short time can lower a score.

## Errors in a report

A wrong entry (a loan that isn't yours, a payment shown as late) can be disputed with the CIC, which checks with the lender. RBI's rules set time limits for resolving such disputes.

## Points to keep in mind

- A settled loan (paid less than owed, by agreement) is reported differently from a closed loan and can lower a score.
- Checking your own report does not lower your score.
- Being a guarantor makes that loan appear in your report too.
