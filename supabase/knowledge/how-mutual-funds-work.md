A mutual fund pools money from many investors and invests it in shares, bonds or other assets according to the scheme's stated objective. A fund manager makes the investment decisions. Each investor owns units of the scheme in proportion to what they put in.

## NAV and units

The net asset value (NAV) is the value of one unit: the scheme's assets minus its liabilities and expenses, divided by the number of units. It is worked out at the end of each business day.

When you invest, you get units at the applicable NAV. When you redeem, you get the NAV of that day for each unit sold.

## A worked example

- You invest ₹10,000 when the NAV is ₹25: ₹10,000 ÷ 25 = 400 units.
- Later the NAV is ₹27.50: 400 × 27.50 = ₹11,000.
- If the NAV falls to ₹22, the same units are worth 400 × 22 = ₹8,800.

A low NAV does not make a scheme cheap, and a high NAV does not make it expensive; what matters is how the NAV changes.

## Expense ratio

The expense ratio is the scheme's yearly running cost as a percentage of its assets. It is not billed separately; it is taken out of the scheme every day, so the NAV is already net of it. At 1% a year, an investment of ₹1,00,000 bears about ₹1,000 of costs over a year.

## Other terms

- Exit load: a charge some schemes apply if units are redeemed within a stated period.
- Scheme documents: the scheme information document and key information memorandum describe the objective, risks and costs.
- Market risk: the value of units can go down as well as up; returns are not guaranteed.
